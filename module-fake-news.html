Understanding Fake News

Learn the anatomy of a false story, how it spreads, and the critical red flags you need to watch out for.

What is Fake News?

Fake news refers to false or misleading information presented as legitimate news. It is often created to damage the reputation of a person or entity, or to make money through advertising revenue.

Key Definition

Disinformation is intentionally false and spread to deceive. Misinformation is false but spread by people who believe it to be true.

How Fake News Spreads

📝

1. False Story Created

A sensational, fabricated story is published on a website designed to look like legitimate news.

🔗

2. Shared on Social Media

Bots or individuals share the link in groups where people are likely to agree with the premise.

⚙️

3. Algorithm Amplifies

Social media algorithms notice the high engagement (outrage, comments) and push the post to more feeds.

🌍

4. Goes Viral

Mainstream users see the story, believe it, and share it further, giving it unwarranted credibility.

Red Flags to Watch For

Use this checklist before you hit "share".

Sensational Headline

Headlines using ALL CAPS or extreme language designed to trigger emotion.

No Named Author

Legitimate journalism almost always attributes articles to a specific reporter.

Odd Website URL

Look out for domains ending in ".com.co" or variations of well-known news sites.

No Corroborating Sources

If it's a massive breaking story, more than one outlet should be covering it.

Key Takeaways

  • Always check the source and author before sharing.
  • Be wary of intense emotional language in headlines.
  • Use fact-checking websites like Snopes or PolitiFact if unsure.
